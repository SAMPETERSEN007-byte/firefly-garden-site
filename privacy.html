Firefly Garden

Privacy Policy

Nothing is shared unless you say yes.

No account. No advertising. No tracking across apps or websites. If you agree, Firefly Garden sends anonymous play stats, such as which level you finished and how many moves it took, so I can make the game better. If you do not, it sends nothing.

Effective 14 September 2026 · applies to Firefly Garden 1.0 (build 4) and later

The short version. Your progress and settings stay on your device. The first time you play, the game asks once whether you want to share anonymous usage. If you say yes, it sends events such as which level you finished, in how many moves and seconds, to PostHog, the analytics service I use. They are filed under a random ID that is not linked to your name, your email address or any account. You can switch sharing off in Settings at any time, and it stops straight away.

What changed

This page used to say Firefly Garden collects nothing. That was true until build 3, which added PostHog product analytics and had it switched on by default. Build 4 changes that, and this version of the policy describes it:

  • Sharing is now opt-in. Until you agree, the analytics SDK is not even started, so nothing is sent.
  • The first time you play, right after the short three-step practice, the game asks you once. Either answer starts the game. If you never answer, for example because you leave the practice early, sharing stays off.
  • If you played an earlier build, sharing starts off in build 4 until you switch it on.
  • You can change your answer any time in Settings, under Share anonymous usage.

What stays on your device

  • Which clearings you have unlocked and completed, and your best number of moves for each.
  • The board you are part-way through, so you can close the app and come back to it.
  • Your Daily Glow history and streak, and your Endless Night progress.
  • Your settings: sound, haptics, reduced motion, and whether you share anonymous usage.

This is stored using the operating system's ordinary app storage. It is not readable by other apps, and the game never sends it anywhere: the events described below report counts such as moves and seconds, never the board or the moves themselves. On iOS this save is kept out of iCloud backups. On Android, the phone's automatic Google backup can include it. Deleting the app deletes it from your device.

What is shared, and only if you say yes

While sharing is on, the game sends these events as you play:

  • level_started: the mode, world and level, its difficulty, and whether it is a replay.
  • level_completed: the mode, world and level, moves, seconds, hints used, and whether it was a replay.
  • level_abandoned: the mode, world and level, and the moves and seconds so far.
  • hint_used: the mode, world and level.
  • world_unlocked: which world.
  • daily_glow_started and daily_glow_completed: the date and whether it is a replay, plus moves, seconds and hints used when you finish.
  • endless_started and endless_ended: the depth and night when you begin; the depth, levels cleared, nights lit and seconds when you stop.
  • settings_changed: which setting you changed, and whether you turned it on or off.
  • analytics_disabled: sent once, at the moment you switch sharing off.
  • The PostHog SDK's own app events: Application Installed, Application Updated (with the previous version), Application Opened (with the link that opened the app, if there was one), Application Became Active, and Application Backgrounded.

Every event also carries:

  • Firefly Garden's name, version and build, the platform (iOS or Android), and a tag saying whether it came from a development or a production build.
  • A random ID that the PostHog SDK creates on your device, and a session ID. The random ID is not the advertising identifier, is not linked to your name, email address, Apple ID or any other account, and is removed when you delete the app.
  • Details the SDK adds itself: the app's bundle identifier; the device type, which the SDK reports as “Mobile” on phones and tablets; the device maker; the operating system and its version; whether the app is running in a simulator; your language and region setting and time zone; the screen's width and height; the SDK's own name and version; the time of the event; and a few flags PostHog uses for its own bookkeeping.

The SDK would also attach your phone's model, but the game removes it before anything is sent, and your phone's name is never sent.

Each time the app starts with sharing on, the SDK also asks PostHog for its settings. That request can carry the same random ID and some of the app and system details listed above.

Like any internet service, PostHog receives your device's IP address with every request, and it may store that address with the events. Every event is marked so that PostHog does not look up a location from it, and I do not use it to identify anyone.

Who receives it

PostHog, Inc., the product analytics service I use, is the only company the game sends anything to. Events go to PostHog's US cloud at us.i.posthog.com and are stored in the United States.

PostHog handles this data for me as a service provider. Under the U.S. privacy-law addendum in its privacy policy, which applies as soon as it starts processing data for me, PostHog may use or disclose that personal information only to provide its service to me or as the law allows or requires, and it commits to helping me answer requests to access or delete it. PostHog also takes part in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and publishes a Data Processing Agreement and a list of the companies it uses to run its service.

Those limits match the ones this page sets for me, with one difference I would rather state than bury: PostHog's terms let it use customer data to develop its own products and machine-learning models, once that data has been aggregated or de-identified so that it cannot reasonably be used to identify anyone. It does not let other companies train models on it.

What is never collected

  • No account. There is no sign-up, no login, no name, and no email address.
  • No advertising and no tracking. There are no ads, no use of the advertising identifier, no App Tracking Transparency prompt, and no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No session replay, autocapture, or crash tracking. Nothing records your screen, taps and screens are not captured automatically, and the app has no crash or error tracking.
  • No location, contacts, photos, microphone, or camera. The app never asks for these permissions because it never uses them.
  • Not your board or your moves. The board and your move history never leave your device; events carry only counts such as moves and seconds.

How long events are kept, and deleting them

Events are kept only so I can maintain and improve the game, and they are used for nothing else.

Because an event carries a random ID rather than anything that names you, I cannot find one person's events from a name or an email address. What you can always do is stop them: switching sharing off stops collection immediately, and deleting the app removes the random ID from your device.

For questions about this data, or to ask for it to be deleted, email sampetersen007@gmail.com.

Changing your mind

Open Settings and use the Share anonymous usage switch. Turning it on starts sharing. Turning it off stops it at once: the game sends one last event, analytics_disabled, so I can count how many people opt out, and then nothing more.

Children

Firefly Garden is a quiet logic puzzle suitable for any age. It never asks anyone for a name, age, or email address. Anonymous usage is shared only if the player says yes and carries no name or contact details, and I use it only to maintain and improve the game, never for advertising or profiling.

What the app stores do see

Apple and Google run the stores the app is distributed through, and they report aggregate figures to me as the developer — roughly how many people downloaded the app, crash reports, and anonymous retention percentages. That collection is done by the platform, not by Firefly Garden, and it is governed by their policies, not this one. I cannot see who you are in any of it.

You can limit what your device shares with Apple under Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, and with Google under Settings → Google → Ads.

If this changes again

If a future version of Firefly Garden collects anything more, or uses it differently, this page will be updated before that version ships, the effective date at the top will change, and the change will be described here rather than quietly folded in.
